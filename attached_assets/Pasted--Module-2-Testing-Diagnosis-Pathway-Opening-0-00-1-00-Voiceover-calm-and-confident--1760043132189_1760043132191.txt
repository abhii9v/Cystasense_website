🎥 Module 2: Testing & Diagnosis Pathway

🎙️ Voiceover (calm and confident)

1. Why Diagnosis Matters (1:00 – 2:30)

Getting the right diagnosis is the first step to effective care. PCOS isn’t just about irregular periods — it’s a complex hormonal condition that varies from person to person.
An accurate diagnosis helps rule out other disorders and ensures treatment is tailored to your needs.

2. Understanding the Criteria (2:30 – 5:00)

For years, doctors have used the Rotterdam criteria, which require two of the following three features:

Irregular or absent ovulation (meaning irregular periods)

High levels of androgens, either on a blood test or through symptoms like acne or excess hair growth

Polycystic ovaries seen on an ultrasound

In 2023, the guidelines added clarity — especially for adolescents. For younger patients, it’s normal to have irregular cycles after puberty, so the focus is on persistent symptoms rather than quick labeling.

For adults, the diagnosis remains based on combining symptoms, test results, and careful exclusion of other causes.

3. The Role of Blood Tests (5:00 – 7:00)

Blood tests help assess hormone levels. Common ones include:

LH and FSH ratio: often elevated in PCOS.

Testosterone and DHEAS: check for androgen excess.

AMH (Anti-Müllerian Hormone): often higher in PCOS, reflecting more follicles in the ovaries.

Thyroid and prolactin: done to exclude other causes of irregular cycles.

It’s important to remember: one abnormal result doesn’t confirm PCOS — doctors look at the whole picture, including your story and symptoms.

4. Ultrasound Explained (7:00 – 9:00)

An ultrasound helps visualize the ovaries. In PCOS, the ovaries may appear slightly enlarged and show multiple small follicles — often described as a “string of pearls.”

But newer guidelines emphasize that ultrasound is not always required — especially if you already meet the other two diagnostic criteria.
So, if your doctor doesn’t order an ultrasound, don’t worry — it may simply not be necessary for you.

5. The Emotional Side of Testing (9:00 – 11:00)

Getting tested for PCOS can feel overwhelming — with multiple blood draws, confusing reports, and long medical terms.
Remember: testing isn’t about labeling you — it’s about understanding your body’s unique rhythm.

If your results feel unclear, it’s okay to ask for explanations. You have the right to understand what’s happening inside your body.

6. What Happens After Diagnosis (11:00 – 13:00)

Once diagnosed, your doctor will discuss management plans — from lifestyle guidance to medications, depending on your goals: fertility, metabolic health, or symptom control.
Each journey is unique, and treatment evolves as your needs do.

We’ll dive deeper into those strategies in the next module — Nutrition, Lifestyle, and PCOS Management.
